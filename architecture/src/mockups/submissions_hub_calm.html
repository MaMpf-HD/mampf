Splitting the head cost the standing its permanent place at the top. The strip above is what buys it back: one line — points, bar, threshold, pending — that latches to the top once the standing block has scrolled out of view.

“Something has come in” — two facts per sheet, never one

A sheet has no single state “corrected”. The scan — the tutor's handwriting, with points and remarks on it — belongs to the submission, so to the whole team. The points are typed into MaMpf separately and belong to one person. They rarely arrive together, and both orders are normal: the scans go up and the points follow days later, or the points are read off the sheets in the tutorial and the scans follow. So the page never infers one from the other, and “new” does not mean “corrected” — it means something has changed since you last looked that you are allowed to see. That one rule covers all five cases below, corrections to earlier results included. It is told twice, and both places belong to the list: a news row above it, and a marker in the row's left margin. The standing block is deliberately not one of them — it is grey and unframed so that it does not pull the eye, which makes it the worst possible home for a message.

How the marker goes. Opening the row clears it, or “mark all as seen” clears the lot — and only for you — it is kept per person and per submission, so a partner reading the correction does not take your marker away. One line per condition, in the words the rule was written in — never one computed verdict. A condition that is met and one that is still open read the same, because neither has cost anything. Every state a row can be in

Two columns carry the state and never both speak at once. The points column holds a number whenever there is one; the status column holds a badge only when there cannot be — grey (nothing to do yet), amber (you have to act), red (this cost you points), never green. A quiet line under the sheet name explains, and only when something needs explaining. Every number also carries an invisible label, so the colour is never the only thing saying what happened.
